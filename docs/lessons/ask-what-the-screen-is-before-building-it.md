# Ask what it looks like before building it

**Summary:** A grill-me round about a feature's rules does not settle what the player sees; ask
for that picture too, before the view is built — and let him pick looks by seeing them.

## Where it comes from

Carried over from Chief Wants His Cut (and Hollowdeep): a camp was grilled in nine forks, all about
rules, and built as a screen of cards; only when it got pictures did it come out that a walkable
map had been pictured all along. The rules survived; the scene was replaced. Looks picked from
file names or descriptions were rejected on sight; a contact sheet or an in-game frame settled
each choice in one step.

## How it applies here

- Without frames (ADR 001) every new effect needs an answer to "how is it seen?" — a mustard
  puddle, a sneeze, a sticky cheese, a recipe found. Ask one fork about that per feature in the
  grill, before the first view.
- The look question of M4 (the drawn stand-ins or GPT pictures) is decided from candidates side
  by side, not from words.
- "Passt" after playing confirms that it works, not that it is what he pictured.
